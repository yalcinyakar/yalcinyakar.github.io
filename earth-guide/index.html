001Arrival · Visitor protocol

Observe before contact

+

There is no verified extraterrestrial visitor protocol. This guide proposes a conservative one: observe at a distance, announce peaceful intent and request consent before approaching living beings. Do not assume that familiar-looking air, liquids or food are compatible with your biology. A human-built environment is designed around human bodies, senses and tolerances.

002Arrival · Visitor protocol

A living world, not an empty landing site

+

A quiet surface can still contain organisms, nests, roots and microbial communities. Avoid releasing unfamiliar organisms, chemicals or waste. Do not collect living specimens without permission. Treat both your own biology and Earth's biology as untested interaction partners. These are proposed visitor precautions, not evidence of contact between civilizations.

003Arrival · Human convention

No single planetary spokesperson

+

Earth has many political jurisdictions, languages and institutions. No person automatically speaks for all humans. A local permission does not establish global consent. Identify the people affected by an action and the authorities responsible for the location. The absence of an answer is not permission.

004Planet · Measured

The address

+

Earth is the third planet from the Sun, a star in the Milky Way. It has one natural moon. Its average orbital distance is about 150 million kilometres. Earth is about 4.54 billion years old. The astronomical neighbourhood is physical; names such as Earth, Sun and Milky Way are human labels.

005Planet · Measured

A rotating clock

+

One solar day is approximately 24 hours. One orbit takes about 365.25 days. Earth's rotation produces the day–night cycle; its tilted axis produces seasons as the planet orbits the Sun. The northern and southern hemispheres have opposite seasonal cycles. Seasons are not caused primarily by changing distance from the Sun.

006Planet · Measured

Air is a mixture

+

Dry air near the surface is roughly 78% nitrogen and 21% oxygen, with other gases making up the remainder. Water vapour varies. Pressure and density change with altitude and weather. These values describe Earth's atmosphere; they do not establish that it is breathable for an unknown organism.

007Physics · Established model

Mass is not weight

+

Mass measures inertia. Weight is the gravitational force on that mass. Near Earth's surface, gravitational acceleration is approximately 9.8 metres per second squared, with location-dependent variation. A mass of one kilogram therefore weighs about 9.8 newtons here. Falling objects also encounter air resistance; shape and speed matter.

008Physics · Established model

Energy changes form

+

Energy can be transferred or transformed rather than created from nothing in ordinary closed-system accounting. Motion, heat, chemical reactions and electrical systems are connected through energy exchange. Real machines dissipate useful energy, often as heat. A device claiming unlimited work without an energy source conflicts with tested physical principles.

009Physics · Established model

Signals take time

+

Light in vacuum travels at exactly 299,792,458 metres per second in the SI system. Sound is a mechanical disturbance and needs a medium. It does not cross empty space. Electrical and optical communications also have propagation delays. A distant observation describes an earlier event, not instantaneous access to its present.

010Chemistry · Established model

Atoms, molecules, reactions

+

Elements are distinguished by the number of protons in their atoms. Atoms can form molecules and larger structures through chemical bonding. A chemical reaction rearranges atoms and changes bonds. Changing an element itself is a nuclear process. Composition alone is insufficient: structure, concentration, temperature and pressure can change a material's behaviour.

011Chemistry · Established model

Water has conditions

+

Water is H₂O. At approximately one atmosphere, pure water freezes near 0 °C and boils near 100 °C. Dissolved substances and pressure alter these boundaries. A transparent liquid is not necessarily water. Water containing dissolved salts is chemically different from pure water and may interact differently with organisms and equipment.

012Chemistry · Established model

A small amount can matter

+

Chemical effects depend on dose, concentration, exposure route and duration. Colour, smell or natural origin does not establish safety. Acidity and alkalinity affect reactions; the pH scale is logarithmic. Some substances react vigorously when mixed. Identify materials before contact and leave unfamiliar mixtures to qualified local specialists.

013Oceans & land · Measured

Mostly ocean

+

The ocean covers approximately 71% of Earth's surface. Its connected waters are divided into named regions by humans. The ocean stores and transports heat, supports diverse ecosystems and interacts with the atmosphere. Surface appearance conceals depth, currents and pressure. The sea is not simply empty space between landmasses.

014Oceans & land · Established model

Water keeps moving

+

Evaporation, condensation, precipitation and flow connect oceans, air, soils, rivers and underground water. Ice and snow also store water. Human withdrawals and land changes affect this cycle. A river's visible flow is only one part of the system; groundwater can connect locations that seem separate at the surface.

015Oceans & land · Established model

The ground is mobile

+

Earth's outer rigid shell is divided into moving tectonic plates. Their interactions produce many earthquakes, mountain ranges and volcanic systems. Continents are not permanent arrangements. Plate boundaries help explain hazard patterns, but earthquakes also occur away from them. A map is a description of a changing planet.

016Life · Established model

Cells are the working units

+

Known cellular life uses cells: bounded chemical systems that exchange matter and energy. Some organisms contain one cell; others contain many specialised cells. Bacteria and archaea are distinct from organisms with complex nucleated cells. Viruses depend on host cells to replicate, so their status as living entities is debated.

017Life · Established model

Inheritance is editable history

+

DNA stores hereditary information in known cellular life. Mutation, selection, genetic drift and gene flow contribute to evolutionary change. Evolution is not a ladder leading inevitably to humans. Organisms share ancestry and are adapted to particular circumstances; a useful trait in one environment can be costly in another.

018Life · Established model

Networks, not isolated species

+

Organisms interact through feeding, competition, cooperation and decomposition. Photosynthesis supplies energy to many ecosystems; some systems depend on chemical energy instead. Removing a species or changing a habitat can affect many others. A landscape that looks unoccupied to a large visitor may still be ecologically active.

019Humans · Established model

One species, many experiences

+

Humans are Homo sapiens, social primates with unusually extensive symbolic communication and cumulative technology. Individuals differ in abilities, history, identity and needs. A face, nationality or group label does not reliably tell you what a person believes. Learn from individuals without treating one encounter as a description of an entire population.

020Humans · Human convention

Consent is an ongoing choice

+

Human rights frameworks recognise dignity and equal rights. Ask before touching, recording, taking possessions or entering private spaces. Consent to one action does not imply consent to another. People can withdraw agreement. Practices and enforcement differ, but convenience or curiosity should not override another person's agency.

021Humans · Established model

Bodies need maintenance

+

Human bodies depend on suitable oxygen supply, water, nutrients, temperature regulation and rest. Human health knowledge does not automatically apply to unfamiliar life. Do not infer compatibility from shared appearance or chemistry. In a human emergency, seek trained local responders rather than extrapolating an alien treatment from this general guide.

022Communication · Human convention

Language is more than a dictionary

+

Humans communicate through speech, writing, sign languages, gesture and technology. Meaning depends on context and community. Tone, irony and idioms can reverse a literal reading. Begin with simple statements, establish shared definitions and ask the receiver to confirm understanding. No natural human language is a universal planetary language.

023Communication · Human convention

Agree on a reference frame

+

The International System of Units provides standard units for time, length, mass and other quantities. Give units alongside numbers. State the coordinate system, time reference and uncertainty when sharing measurements. A bare number such as 100 is ambiguous; it might describe temperature, distance, a count or a local convention.

024Communication · Human convention

Maps are agreements about location

+

Latitude and longitude are common angular coordinates on Earth. Accurate positions also depend on a geodetic reference and often altitude. North on a map is a convention, not the direction of importance. Boundaries, place names and maps may reflect contested claims. Verify local navigation information rather than treating every map as neutral.

025Civilization · Human convention

Borders are not natural laws

+

States, laws and institutions organise much human activity. Their rules vary by location and can change. Ownership, travel permissions and protected areas are not visible from orbit. This guide is orientation, not legal permission. Contact the relevant local institution before activities that affect people, territory or shared infrastructure.

026Civilization · Human convention

Money coordinates exchange

+

Humans use money, prices, credit and contracts to organise exchange. Value is not an intrinsic property of a banknote or digital number; it depends on institutions and collective trust. Access to resources is uneven. A listed price does not capture every environmental or social cost, and agreement requires informed participants.

027Civilization · Human convention

Cooperation and conflict coexist

+

Humans cooperate through families, communities, governments and international organisations. They also compete and wage wars. Technology alone does not predict peaceful behaviour. Distinguish stated ideals, institutional rules and actual conduct. The Universal Declaration of Human Rights is a shared normative reference, not evidence that those rights are always respected.

028Knowledge · Method

Ask how we know

+

Scientific claims are evaluated through evidence, measurement, testable explanations and criticism. Reproducibility and independent checks strengthen confidence. A model can be useful within a domain without being final or complete. Look for methods, uncertainty and alternative explanations rather than treating confidence, popularity or authority as proof.

029Knowledge · Method

Mathematics is a shared tool

+

Mathematics describes structures, quantities and relationships through defined symbols and rules. Human notation is conventional; the relationship can be expressed in other notations. Distinguish a mathematical result from a claim about the physical world: applying an equation requires assumptions and evidence that the model fits the situation.

030Knowledge · Open question

The record has gaps

+

Humanity has not solved every problem. The origin of life, the nature of consciousness and many features of the universe remain subjects of research. An unanswered question is not proof of any preferred explanation. This archive is an expandable introduction with sources, not a complete copy of human knowledge.

031Technology · Established model

Infrastructure is interdependent

+

Electricity, transport, water systems, computing and communication networks support many human settlements. Disrupting one can affect others. A device's usefulness does not reveal its dependencies. Seek permission before connecting equipment to infrastructure; observe voltage, frequency, communication protocol and access rules rather than assuming universal compatibility.

032Technology · Established model

The internet is not one mind

+

The internet connects many independently operated networks. Services exchange data using agreed protocols. Information online may be accurate, mistaken, manipulated or private. Being able to access a system does not grant permission to change it. Shared links and messages should respect privacy, identity and the recipient's consent.

033Technology · Human convention

Space is shared responsibility

+

Human space activities are influenced by international treaties and national rules. The Outer Space Treaty provides foundational principles for participating states. These frameworks were written for human institutions; they are not a verified agreement with extraterrestrial civilizations. Orbital activity can affect other spacecraft and people on the ground.

034Culture · Human convention

Art carries more than information

+

Music, visual art, literature, dance and other practices communicate emotion, identity and memory. Their meaning may rely on history and community. There is no single correct human aesthetic. Ask what a work means to its makers and audience before translating it into your own cultural categories.

035Culture · Human convention

Belief is not uniform

+

Humans hold religious, philosophical and secular worldviews. Rituals and symbols can have deep significance. A community's belief should be described respectfully without presenting it as a measured physical fact. Avoid assuming that agreement with one proposition establishes agreement with an entire tradition.

036Culture · Visitor protocol

Keep room for other lives

+

Learning about Earth should not require controlling it. Preserve habitats, respect people's choices and leave shared places usable for others. Document where a claim comes from and correct mistakes openly. For a visitor, successful contact is not just acquiring information; it is learning without imposing avoidable costs.

037Changing Earth · Established model

Climate is not today's weather

+

Weather describes short-term atmospheric conditions. Climate describes statistical patterns over longer periods. Human activities, especially greenhouse-gas emissions, have caused global warming. Impacts differ across regions and ecosystems. A cold day does not disprove a warming climate; the question concerns trends in many measurements over time.

038Changing Earth · Established model

Extinction removes possibilities

+

Species can disappear when populations cannot persist. Habitat changes, exploitation, introduced species and environmental changes can contribute. Loss is more than a reduction in a count: ecological relationships and evolutionary histories are also affected. Conservation must consider both organisms and the people who live alongside them.
