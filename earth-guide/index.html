00 / FIRST CONTACT

First contact.
Try not to make it weird.

No extraterrestrial arrival has been verified. These proposed precautions frame the guide; they are not an established contact protocol.

  1. 01

    Do not lick the planet.

    Check air, pressure and chemistry before opening your helmet. “The locals seem fine” is not a safety assessment.

  2. 02

    That empty field has tenants.

    Roots, nests and microbes may occupy your landing site. At ant altitude, your spacecraft is a housing crisis. Avoid contamination.

  3. 03

    Request permission. Then be impressive.

    Ask before approaching. No human speaks for everyone, although several appear to be auditioning.

001Arrival · Visitor protocol

First contact. Skip the surprise entrance.

+

A dramatic landing makes excellent cinema and a terrible introduction. Start with hello; save the smoke machine for later.

There is no verified extraterrestrial visitor protocol. This guide proposes a conservative one: observe at a distance, announce peaceful intent and request consent before approaching living beings. Do not assume that familiar-looking air, liquids or food are compatible with your biology. A human-built environment is designed around human bodies, senses and tolerances.

That empty field has tenants.

+

From your altitude it looked vacant. At ant altitude, you have just parked on an entire neighbourhood.

A quiet surface can still contain organisms, nests, roots and microbial communities. Avoid releasing unfamiliar organisms, chemicals or waste. Do not collect living specimens without permission. Treat both your own biology and Earth's biology as untested interaction partners. These are proposed visitor precautions, not evidence of contact between civilizations.

004Planet · Measured

Third rock. Blue one. Hard to miss.

+

If you reached Neptune, you have overshot. Our postal system is not prepared for that return address.

Earth is the third planet from the Sun, a star in the Milky Way. It has one natural moon. Its average orbital distance is about 150 million kilometres. Earth is about 4.54 billion years old. The astronomical neighbourhood is physical; names such as Earth, Sun and Milky Way are human labels.

005Planet · Measured

Day and night: the original rotation schedule.

+

The planet turns the lights off without asking. Humans responded by inventing lamps and complaining about mornings.

One solar day is approximately 24 hours. One orbit takes about 365.25 days. Earth's rotation produces the day–night cycle; its tilted axis produces seasons as the planet orbits the Sun. The northern and southern hemispheres have opposite seasonal cycles. Seasons are not caused primarily by changing distance from the Sun.

006Planet · Measured

The atmosphere is not one-size-fits-all.

+

A human taking a deep breath is not a product endorsement for your species. Keep the helmet until you know.

Dry air near the surface is roughly 78% nitrogen and 21% oxygen, with other gases making up the remainder. Water vapour varies. Pressure and density change with altitude and weather. These values describe Earth's atmosphere; they do not establish that it is breathable for an unknown organism.

007Physics · Established model

Gravity: a very committed relationship.

+

Drop your luggage and Earth will accept delivery immediately. Unfortunately, it also accepts your feet.

Mass measures inertia. Weight is the gravitational force on that mass. Near Earth's surface, gravitational acceleration is approximately 9.8 metres per second squared, with location-dependent variation. A mass of one kilogram therefore weighs about 9.8 newtons here. Falling objects also encounter air resistance; shape and speed matter.

008Physics · Established model

Free energy? Read the small print.

+

The universe is notoriously reluctant to fund a perpetual-motion startup.

Energy can be transferred or transformed rather than created from nothing in ordinary closed-system accounting. Motion, heat, chemical reactions and electrical systems are connected through energy exchange. Real machines dissipate useful energy, often as heat. A device claiming unlimited work without an energy source conflicts with tested physical principles.

009Physics · Established model

Even light has a commute.

+

In space, nobody can hear your dramatic entrance. Radio is a much better booking system.

Light in vacuum travels at exactly 299,792,458 metres per second in the SI system. Sound is a mechanical disturbance and needs a medium. It does not cross empty space. Electrical and optical communications also have propagation delays. A distant observation describes an earlier event, not instantaneous access to its present.

010Chemistry · Established model

Atoms: tiny parts, considerable drama.

+

Same ingredients, different arrangement, entirely different result. Chemistry is extremely particular about the seating plan.

Elements are distinguished by the number of protons in their atoms. Atoms can form molecules and larger structures through chemical bonding. A chemical reaction rearranges atoms and changes bonds. Changing an element itself is a nuclear process. Composition alone is insufficient: structure, concentration, temperature and pressure can change a material's behaviour.

011Chemistry · Established model

Water has several ways to ruin a picnic.

+

Ice, liquid, vapour: one molecule with range. A clear puddle is still not a tasting invitation.

Water is H₂O. At approximately one atmosphere, pure water freezes near 0 °C and boils near 100 °C. Dissolved substances and pressure alter these boundaries. A transparent liquid is not necessarily water. Water containing dissolved salts is chemically different from pure water and may interact differently with organisms and equipment.

012Chemistry · Established model

Natural does not mean snackable.

+

Nature also manufactures things you absolutely should not put in your mouth. Its branding department is misleading.

Chemical effects depend on dose, concentration, exposure route and duration. Colour, smell or natural origin does not establish safety. Acidity and alkalinity affect reactions; the pH scale is logarithmic. Some substances react vigorously when mixed. Identify materials before contact and leave unfamiliar mixtures to qualified local specialists.

013Oceans & land · Measured

Mostly water. Mysteriously named Earth.

+

Naming a mostly ocean-covered planet “Earth” tells you something about the committee that named it.

The ocean covers approximately 71% of Earth's surface. Its connected waters are divided into named regions by humans. The ocean stores and transports heat, supports diverse ecosystems and interacts with the atmosphere. Surface appearance conceals depth, currents and pressure. The sea is not simply empty space between landmasses.

014Oceans & land · Established model

The water is on another business trip.

+

That cloud is not staying. Neither is the river. Earth runs a very ambitious water-recycling programme.

Evaporation, condensation, precipitation and flow connect oceans, air, soils, rivers and underground water. Ice and snow also store water. Human withdrawals and land changes affect this cycle. A river's visible flow is only one part of the system; groundwater can connect locations that seem separate at the surface.

015Oceans & land · Established model

The floor is moving. Slowly. Usually.

+

The continents are rearranging the furniture. They did not ask the cities first.

Earth's outer rigid shell is divided into moving tectonic plates. Their interactions produce many earthquakes, mountain ranges and volcanic systems. Continents are not permanent arrangements. Plate boundaries help explain hazard patterns, but earthquakes also occur away from them. A map is a description of a changing planet.

016Life · Established model

Cells: the very small workforce.

+

Your human host is an enormous cellular collaboration wearing shoes.

Known cellular life uses cells: bounded chemical systems that exchange matter and energy. Some organisms contain one cell; others contain many specialised cells. Bacteria and archaea are distinct from organisms with complex nucleated cells. Viruses depend on host cells to replicate, so their status as living entities is debated.

017Life · Established model

Evolution did not have a five-year plan.

+

No finish line, no final boss, no certificate for becoming human. Biology is not a promotion ladder.

DNA stores hereditary information in known cellular life. Mutation, selection, genetic drift and gene flow contribute to evolutionary change. Evolution is not a ladder leading inevitably to humans. Organisms share ancestry and are adapted to particular circumstances; a useful trait in one environment can be costly in another.

019Humans · Established model

Humans: same species, different settings.

+

Meeting one grumpy human does not establish that the planet is hostile. You may simply have arrived before breakfast.

Humans are Homo sapiens, social primates with unusually extensive symbolic communication and cumulative technology. Individuals differ in abilities, history, identity and needs. A face, nationality or group label does not reliably tell you what a person believes. Learn from individuals without treating one encounter as a description of an entire population.

Humans communicate through speech, writing, sign languages, gesture and technology. Meaning depends on context and community. Tone, irony and idioms can reverse a literal reading. 040Chemistry · Measured / established model

Ice floats. The lake declined a solid reboot.

+

Visitor, the frozen roof is a physics feature, not a parking permit. Humans saw floating ice and somehow proposed skating on it.

The facts: Density is mass per unit volume. Near ordinary atmospheric pressure, pure liquid water reaches its greatest density at about 4 °C; cooling it further toward freezing makes it less dense. Ordinary ice has a more open molecular arrangement than liquid water, so it floats. In freshwater lakes, this density pattern helps colder water remain above denser water as the surface approaches freezing. Liquid water can persist below a frozen surface; this does not mean every lake stays partly liquid. Dissolved salts change density, so do not copy the freshwater rule blindly into an ocean model.

Proposed visitor precaution: Observe from shore until local conditions are assessed. Floating ice is not proof that it can support your landing gear. That recommendation is ours; the density measurements are not a joke.

041Life · Established model

DNA: four letters, several billion plot twists.

+

Earth life stores instructions in a four-letter alphabet, then spends entire careers arguing about the footnotes. The alphabet is compact; the debugging is not.

The facts: Known cellular life on Earth stores hereditary information in DNA, whose sequence uses four nucleotide bases: adenine, thymine, cytosine and guanine. In gene expression, cells transcribe information from DNA into RNA and ribosomes translate RNA codons into amino-acid sequences that form proteins. The genetic code is nearly universal across Earth species, with minor exceptions—strong evidence of shared ancestry, not proof that every organism has the same genes or traits. Cells also regulate which genes are expressed, when and how much; carrying a gene is not the same as constantly using it.

Proposed visitor precaution: Do not assume your biology uses DNA, our codon table or proteins at all. Sample nothing and exchange no biological material until compatibility is tested. That caution is hypothetical; the molecular biology above describes observed Earth life.

042Life · Ecosystem model

Dead is not done. It has a recycling appointment.

+

Earth has no planetary cleanup crew, so fungi and bacteria took the contract. Payment remains mostly theoretical.

The facts: Energy and matter behave differently in ecosystems. Energy enters food webs—commonly as sunlight captured by photosynthetic organisms—and flows through trophic levels, with part dispersed as heat at each transfer. Matter is conserved and recycled through organisms and the environment. Decomposers and detritivores process dead organic material; bacteria, fungi and invertebrates are common participants in detrital food webs. Their activity helps return chemical nutrients to forms that can re-enter living systems. “Dead material” is therefore not ecologically inactive or disposable scenery.

Proposed visitor precaution: Do not sterilize soil, remove fallen wood or clear leaf litter merely because it looks untidy. First determine which organisms and nutrient cycles use it. Human-made waste is a different category and should not be mistaken for habitat. The caution is ours; the energy-flow and nutrient-cycle model is established Earth ecology.

043Oceans & land · Measured

The ocean is not a very large swimming pool.

+

It is salty, pressurized and almost entirely lacking in lifeguards. Humanity looked at this and invented recreational diving.

The facts: Open-ocean seawater has an average salinity of about 35 parts per thousand, although salinity varies with evaporation, precipitation, freshwater input and location. Much of the dissolved material ultimately comes from weathering of rocks on land and chemical exchanges at the seafloor. Depth adds another constraint: water pressure rises by roughly one atmosphere for every 10 metres below the surface, in addition to the atmosphere already pressing at sea level. Native marine organisms tolerate particular combinations of salinity, pressure, temperature and dissolved gases; those tolerances are not transferable to an unknown visitor.

Proposed visitor precaution: Treat every descent as entry into a changing pressure vessel. Use equipment rated for the intended depth and test both fluids and biological compatibility before exposure. “The locals swim there” is observational data, not certification for your anatomy.

044Oceans & land · Geodetic model

Every flat map lies. The useful ones disclose how.

+

Humans flattened a round planet onto rectangles, lost several geometric properties and called the result “directions.” Surprisingly, it often works.

The facts: A map projection converts locations on Earth's curved surface to a flat map. That conversion cannot preserve area, shape, distance and direction everywhere at once. Different projections deliberately protect different properties. A Mercator map is useful for representing direction, but greatly enlarges areas and distances toward the poles. Equal-area projections preserve relative area while distorting shapes. Distortion also depends on map scale and the region displayed, so there is no universally best projection—only one suited to a stated purpose.

Proposed visitor precaution: Before navigating, ask for the projection, scale and geodetic reference instead of trusting the picture alone. Never estimate landing distance, territory size or polar clearance from an unlabeled world map. The geometry is established; the landing rule is ours.

045Humans · Historical reconstruction

Agriculture was not a software update.

+

Humanity did not receive Farming 1.0 on the same morning. Several communities developed it independently; others reviewed the workload and declined.

The evidence-based reconstruction: The shift from foraging toward farming and animal domestication was gradual, uneven and independently developed in several regions, including Southwest Asia, China, sub-Saharan Africa, Mesoamerica and South America. Different communities domesticated different species. Reliable food surpluses could support population growth, permanent settlements and specialised labour, but agriculture also demanded substantial work and contributed to new social hierarchies. Many societies continued hunting, gathering, herding or mixed strategies because farming was not practical or preferable everywhere.

Visitor note: Do not describe living foragers, pastoralists or small farmers as earlier “levels” of humanity. They are contemporary societies using strategies shaped by local ecology, history and choice. The historical pattern is reconstructed from archaeological evidence; the etiquette is our recommendation.
